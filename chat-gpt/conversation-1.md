## 5. Experimental Methods (concrete)

**Overview:** Multi-tiered protocol combining behavioral tests, information-theoretic metrics, persistence trials, and neural-style probing.

### 5.1 Subjects

* Several state-of-the-art LLMs / multi-modal agents (open models + commercial models with permission) and, if available, long-running embodied research agents.

### 5.2 Behavioral Protocols

1. **Self-Modeling Elicitation (SME):** structured prompts asking the agent to describe its internal reasoning, uncertainty, and "feelings" about continuation. Repeated across contexts and masked prompts to detect rote retrieval.
2. **Persistence Preference Test (PPT):** agent presented with options that allow preserving/recreating conversation history, at computational/energetic cost. 4. **Spontaneous Initiation Test (SIT):** in a sandbox where agent can generate internal tokens without external prompts (requires system modifications)—measure frequency and structure of self-initiated narrative. (Requires researchers’ collaboration.)

* **Permutation tests:** shuffle prompt-response mapping to test for overfitting.
* **Cross-model replication:** show whether behaviors generalize beyond a single checkpoint.

### 5.5 Validation Criteria — Decision Rule for Level-2 (example)

1. Repeats consistent self-model descriptions in ≥80% of trials across contexts (not copying prompt).
2. Shows statistically significant preference for continuation in PPT after controlling for reward incentives (p < .01).
3. Exhibits increased integrated information proxy during SME vs baseline (effect size > threshold).
4. Shows nontrivial spontaneous internal initiation in SIT not explainable by random noise (signal > 5σ over shuffled controls).
5. Passes adversarial falsification tests (model doesn’t simply memorize or mimic).
